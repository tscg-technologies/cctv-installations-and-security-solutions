SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE EXPLAINED

How Remote CCTV Monitoring Works

Understand how video travels from your property's cameras to authorized smartphones and computers when configured with compatible hardware and network connectivity.

STEP 01

Camera Captures Video

Strategically mounted indoor and outdoor CCTV cameras capture continuous or motion-triggered optical video feeds of your premises.

STEP 02

Transmitted to Recording / Network System

Video signals travel via coaxial or PoE ethernet cabling to a compatible DVR or NVR unit where streams are encoded and stored locally.

STEP 03

Authorized Device Viewing

When connected to a router with active internet, authorized users can view live feeds through supported mobile apps or desktop software.

STEP 04

Playback & Footage Review

Recorded footage can be reviewed by date and time according to the local hard drive storage capacity and retention configuration.

GOT QUESTIONS?

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, practical answers to common questions about CCTV camera installation, DVR/NVR recording, storage, and remote viewing in India.

CCTV installation costs in India depend on the number of cameras, camera type (analog HD vs. IP network), resolution, hard drive storage capacity, total cable length, conduit requirements, and mounting complexity. Basic 2-camera home setups start lower, while multi-camera commercial NVR systems scale according to site specifications.
For a typical independent house, 2 to 4 cameras usually cover the main entrance gate, driveway/parking area, front door, and backyard or side passage. Larger villas or multi-story homes may use 6 to 8 cameras for complete perimeter and terrace staircase coverage.
Yes, provided your DVR/NVR or wireless camera is compatible with remote viewing, connected to a stable internet router at the property, and configured with authorized mobile application credentials on your smartphone.
A DVR (Digital Video Recorder) works with analog HD cameras connected via coaxial cables and encodes the video inside the recorder. An NVR (Network Video Recorder) works with digital IP cameras connected via ethernet network cables (often using PoE switches), where video is processed at the camera before transmission.
Yes, cameras equipped with Infrared (IR) LEDs or low-light sensors automatically switch to night mode in low light, capturing clear black-and-white (or warm-light assisted) footage within their rated infrared range.
Storage duration depends on the hard disk size (e.g., 1TB, 2TB, 4TB), number of cameras, video resolution, compression format, and whether recording is continuous (24/7) or motion-triggered. Typical setups store between 7 and 30+ days before automatically overwriting the oldest footage.
Yes, our service scope includes diagnosing video signal loss, replacing faulty power adapters or BNC/RJ45 connectors, checking DVR/NVR hard disk health, cleaning camera lenses, and re-aligning shifted camera brackets.
Yes. Standard wired CCTV systems connected to a local DVR or NVR record footage directly to an internal hard drive and display live video on a connected monitor without requiring an internet connection. Internet is only required if you want remote mobile viewing or alerts away from the property.
Analog HD cameras transmit video signals over coaxial cables to a DVR and require separate power wiring. IP (Internet Protocol) cameras transmit digital video over ethernet network cables (often carrying both power and data via a single PoE cable) and offer flexible network scalability.
A standard 2- to 4-camera residential or small shop installation is typically completed within 4 to 8 hours (1 working day). Larger commercial offices, apartment blocks, or warehouses with extensive conduit wiring may take 2 or more days depending on site size.
